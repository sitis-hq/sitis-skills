# When to pitch a track in Spotify for Artists: what artists' real experience shows (as of October 2026)

> **Background, not used for planning.** The timings below are artists' reports; the recipes never
> plan by them. Only Spotify's rules (one pitch at a time, a released song cannot be pitched, the
> 500-character description) are used, as stated in `../SKILL.md`.

Short answer: pitch on the same day the track appears in the Upcoming tab. To make that possible, hand the release to your distributor 4–6 weeks before it comes out, and if your profile is new and not yet confirmed, 6–7 weeks before. Space singles at least 4 weeks apart so each one gets its own pitch window. The artists' stories I found all point the same way: the real bottleneck is not "how many weeks editors like", but the chain "distributor → the track appearing in Spotify for Artists → the pitch". It eats 1–3 weeks, and that is where people fail.

## TL;DR

- **Timing.** By people's experience, 7 days is the minimum for Release Radar, not a strategy. Live cases show that even with delivery 21 days ahead, a new artist managed to pitch only on the last permitted day. The goal is to pitch 3–4 weeks ahead and to deliver to the distributor 4–6 weeks ahead (new profile: 6–7).
- **The "one pitch" limit.** The workaround is simple: singles one at a time, 4+ weeks apart, with the next track pitched on the release day of the previous one. Delivering two releases at once is risky: one user reports that because of this they could not pitch one of them.\[1\] For an EP/album, one focus track is pitched, and editors may pick a different one.\[2\]
- **Expectations.** Even an early and careful pitch is no guarantee. There are cases of "pitched 2 and 4 weeks ahead — zero editorial playlists and even problems with Release Radar". According to the Spotify for Artists blog "Behind the Playlists", as of February 2020, since the launch of pitching in 2018 editors had playlisted 72,000 artists and 20% of pitches. The main guaranteed benefit of a pitch is the choice of the track for subscribers' Release Radar.

## An important caveat about the evidence

You asked me to rely on people's experience rather than on articles. I honestly tried, but the search index available in this research almost never returns Reddit, Gearspace and KVR threads. A separate extra search aimed only at Reddit confirmed this: it returned not a single thread. So the personal experience below is taken mostly from **Spotify Community** (the official forum, but the posts there are written by artists themselves) and **iMusician Community** (a forum of the distributor's users). Some of the Spotify Community cases are old (2019–2021); the most recent are from iMusician (June and October 2026). Official Spotify data and blog guides are marked everywhere as **[background, not user experience]**. Where there is no user data at all (seasonality, Discovery Mode, Marquee), I say so directly and do not pass marketing claims off as experience.

## Key findings

1. **The bottleneck is logistics, not editors' taste.** The most detailed recent case is a post by thibaultcroue in iMusician Community from June 5, 2026. A single was delivered on May 23 for a June 13 release, i.e. with a 21-day margin, and with paid express delivery on top. Timeline: delivery to platforms on May 29, the profile URI arrived on June 3, the profile claim was confirmed on June 4. The pitch succeeded with a one-day margin before the 7-day deadline.\[3\] On top of that, the distributor set the release for June 12 instead of the 13th, which "ate" one more day. The author's conclusion: for a new artist the realistic minimum margin is **5–7 weeks**, because the steps go strictly in sequence (delivery up to 7 business days → profile creation up to 2 weeks → claim from a few days to 2 weeks → pitch at least 7 days ahead).\[3\]
2. **Delays happen to experienced users too.** In a post by Aemme in iMusician Community from October 5, 2026, releases for October 16 and 23 are still stuck in the status "Submitted – Waiting for release review". Support cites a "very large volume of releases".\[4\] In other words, you need a margin for the distributor's moderation as well.
3. **An early pitch does not guarantee a result.** In older Spotify Community cases, an artist pitched every single "at least 2 weeks" ahead, with features from well-known artists, and got neither playlists nor emails.\[5\] Another artist delivered and pitched a track 4 weeks ahead and did not get into subscribers' Release Radar at all.\[6\]
4. **Release Radar is the main tangible benefit of a pitch, but it fails too.** There is a case with zero algorithmic streams after a pitch 2 weeks ahead. Spotify support said at the time that a pitch is not required for Release Radar.\[7\]
5. **Landing in a personalized editorial playlist ≠ streams.** Several artists report: the email about the addition arrived, but there are no streams or only a handful; the track is "not visible" in the playlist.\[8\]\[9\]
6. **A track that is already out cannot be pitched.** Users confirm this in practice, including for pitching through the distributor: "the attempt is burned".\[10\]\[11\]\[12\]

## Details by question

### 1. How far ahead of release to pitch and what produced results

**What people say:**

- **A 21-day margin is cutting it close (iMusician Community, thibaultcroue, June 5, 2026).** The track was delivered 21 days ahead, and the pitch went through on the last permitted day. Quote: "This is the best case scenario. If anything had gone slightly wrong at any step, we would not even be here." The author separately notes that a pitch on the last day is probably worse than a pitch several weeks ahead, but honestly writes that they cannot measure it.\[3\] This is a hypothesis, not a measured fact.
- **2 weeks — no result (Spotify Community, thread "Editorial Playlist Luck?", ~2019).** An independent artist put every single into Upcoming "at least 2 weeks ahead of time". Despite features (The Dangerous Summer, Lil Lotus and others), no playlists and no replies.\[5\]
- **2 weeks — no algorithmic streams (Spotify Community, DonSolare, October 2020).** Quote: "I pitched the track through Spotify For Artists two weeks before release". Result: a few thousand streams from independent playlists and "ZERO % from algorithmic playlists… Not in the radio, not in Release radar". Normally he had about 200 algorithmic streams on the first day and 1–7% of streams from algorithmic sources.\[7\]
- **4 weeks — no Release Radar (Spotify Community, thread "[Artists] New song not added to Release Radar?", ~2020).** "I submitted the song 4 weeks before the release date and also pitched it…" — and zero streams from Release Radar. A moderator sent the question to escalation; how it ended is not visible.\[6\]
- **A pitch 1 day before release (Spotify Community, @nuclearmonkey, ~2024–2025).** The artist pitched an album a day before release and asked whether such a pitch would be counted. He did not get a clear answer.\[13\] This shows clearly why the last moment is a bad strategy: with a margin under 7 days even Release Radar is lost.
- **A user's answer in Spotify Community ("How long in advance to submit an Album").** To an artist's question whether a few days would be enough, another user advises allowing about 2 weeks "just to be safe".\[14\]
- **A technical failure with a 3-week margin (Spotify Community).** An artist with a first release 3 weeks out saw the status "Not eligible to pitch – pitch something that's never been streamed before". He was advised to sort it out with the distributor.\[15\] One more argument for a bigger margin: you need time to fix errors.

**What I could not find:** a verifiable user case of the form "pitched 4+ weeks ahead → got into New Music Friday/Fresh Finds, here are the numbers". Claims about 28 days appear only in marketing blogs and are, as a rule, presented without data: for example, Playlist Push (2025) writes "Your pitch should be submitted at least 28 days before release for the best chance of being reviewed and playlisted", and dynamoi and pitchplus say the same. This is **[background, not user experience]**. Compare: the same class of sources also says "2–3 weeks" (theblackturn), "3–4 weeks" (SubmitHub), and "a month ahead" (SAMESOUND).\[16\]\[17\]\[18\]\[19\] There is no single "rule" even among the guides.

**[Background: Spotify's official position]** The help article: pitch "at least 7 days" before release, and then the track will get into subscribers' Release Radar.\[2\]\[11\] On the New Releases page for artists, the advice is to pitch the focus track "at least two weeks before the release date".\[20\] The Playlisting page says that editors receive "thousands of pitches a week – so the earlier you pitch the better".\[21\] A pitch can be edited until the release day, but "there's no guarantee our editors will see the changes".\[11\]\[22\] So the pitch text should be completed right away, not "I'll fix it later".

**A contradiction with the official recommendations:** officially, "7 days is enough for Release Radar". User experience shows that 7 days is a deadline you may physically fail to meet if the release has not yet appeared in Spotify for Artists. So you should plan from the delivery date, not from the pitch date.

### 2. How to live with the "one pitch at a time" limit

**The official rule [background]:** "You can only pitch one song at a time. Once your pitched song goes live, you can pitch another".\[11\] From an EP or album, one song is pitched,\[21\] and "Our editors might pick a different song for their playlists to the one you pitched".\[2\]\[22\]

**What people say:**

- **A 4-week interval works, and there is no mandatory pause between pitches (iMusician Community, thread "Pitching to spotify, single vs EP").** The author planned to pitch single no. 1 and, after its release, single no. 3, which comes out 4 weeks later, and asked about a supposedly mandatory "4–6 weeks" between pitches. A user's answer: there is no mandatory delay between pitches. But in their experience, if Spotify "knows" about two future releases at once, one of them cannot be pitched: "that happened to me".\[1\] Practical conclusion: **do not deliver two future releases at the same time, or do it deliberately**. The next single must be in Upcoming, but it is pitched only after the previous one is out.
- **Singles one at a time are the norm (Spotify Community, thread "am i on a playlist?").** A user's answer: "Most artists release singles one at a time, which also makes it possible to enter each single to editorial playlist consideration". If you release tracks in a batch, it makes more sense to package them as an EP. Then one track is pitched, but editors may pick another.\[23\]
- **The trap of the distributor's paid pitches (iMusician Community).** If you enable "Editorial Playlist Pitching" at iMusician, your own pitch through Spotify for Artists for that release is blocked.\[24\] And if the distributor's curators decline, according to support, "it won't be possible to pitch it again via Spotify for Artists because… the pitching opportunity has already been 'used up'".\[12\] In addition, the iMusician FAQ "What's Editorial Playlist Pitching?" says for this service "Select a digital release date that is at least 20 working days in the future", i.e. 20 business days, not calendar days, and adds: "Two releases by the same artist cannot be in the editorial playlist pitching process simultaneously". This is also "one pitch at a time", only at the intermediary.

**How to build the schedule (my conclusion from the cases and rules):**

| Step | When | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Deliver single A to the distributor | 5–6 weeks before A's release (new profile: 6–7) | margin for moderation, delivery, profile claim |
| Pitch A | on the day A appears in Upcoming | maximum time for editors, guaranteed Release Radar |
| Deliver single B | 4–6 weeks before B's release; with A already released it does not get in the way in Upcoming | B must be in the system by A's release day |
| Pitch B | on A's release day (the slot is freed) | with a 4-week interval B will have ~28 days for review |
| Interval between singles | 4–6 weeks | with a 4-week margin B has exactly 28 days, with no room for error; 5–6 weeks is more reliable |

A calculation from the pitchplus guide **[background]**: with A releasing on October 1 and B on October 29, B can be pitched only from October 1, i.e. exactly 28 days, and any delay of A eats those days.\[25\] For an album: release 2–3 singles before it, each with its own pitch. For the album itself, pitch a track that has not been released yet, because released tracks cannot be pitched.

### 3. Day of the week, time of year, season

**There is practically no user data.** I did not find a single case with numbers of the form "released in December — the pitch did not work" or "worked better in summer".

- The closest is the thread "Debut Single Release – Best Time/Day to Release on Spotify?" in Spotify Community. There users discuss release time in the listener's time zone: for example, 19:00, when people finish work, or a time suited to a genre, say "gym". \[26\] These are opinions, not measured results.
- There is an indirect case from @FuturePunk (Spotify Community): the Future Funk editorial playlist had not been updated for more than 7 months, and later, by his account, "almost a year". Songs released a month before the last update kept gathering an audience for months.\[27\] Conclusion: **the "season" of an individual niche playlist may matter more than the calendar**. If the target playlist is frozen, an early pitch to it will give nothing.

**[Background, not user experience]** Release Radar updates on Fridays. Spotify keeps a track in a listener's Release Radar for up to 4 weeks if they have not yet heard it.\[28\] So a Friday release is practically the standard. December (holidays and year-end roundups) and "releasing alongside big stars" are frequent blog tips, but in the user sources I found there are no verified numbers on them. Treat them as a hypothesis, not a rule.

### 4. The real probability of editorial playlists and the effect of a pitch on the algorithm

**Official numbers [background]:** the Spotify for Artists blog "Behind the Playlists: Your Questions Answered by Our Playlist Editors" says: "Since launching playlist pitching in 2018, as of February 2020, we've playlisted 72,000 artists and playlisted 20% of pitches". This is old statistics; Spotify has not published fresh ones. For Fresh Finds: the Spotify Newsroom piece "Fresh Finds Turns 10" (May 19, 2025) reports that in 2024 more than 18,000 artists from 127 countries got in, and "More than 90% of artists added to Fresh Finds in 2024 used Spotify for Artists' playlist pitching feature"; according to Spotify, these playlists produced "more than 65 million artist discoveries". That is, for independent artists a pitch is practically the mandatory way in. Keep in mind that the "20%" includes any editorial playlists, including small niche and personalized ones, not only New Music Friday.

**What people say about the quality of placements:**

- **Added, but no streams (Spotify Community, @5tarme, ~2024).** "a song of mine was added to a editorial in my genre. I got a email… its been 5 days now and I'm not getting any streams off this list.. The editorial is personalized".\[9\]
- **There are streams, but the track is "not visible" (Spotify Community, thread about "Rustig & Mooi").** The artist saw 2 streams from the playlist but could not find the track in it. Other users explain: personalized editorial playlists show each listener their own selection.\[8\]
- **A problem with the unique link (Spotify Community, playlists Morgenkaffe, Jazz Argentino, Rock Total).** Artists report that they could not get the unique link that puts the track first and is valid for 7 days. For some listeners the track was not in the playlist.\[29\] Practical conclusion: **"got into editorial" today more often means a personalized playlist with a moderate return**, not a guaranteed thousands of streams.

**The algorithm:**

- By DonSolare's experience (2020), even after a pitch 2 weeks ahead there were exactly zero algorithmic streams, although normally there were about 200 on the first day. Support, by his account, replied: "a song doesn't have to be pitched to be eligible for Release Radar", and for listeners who have already heard the track, it is not shown in Release Radar.\[7\]
- **[Background: Spotify help]** If a track is pitched, that very track goes into subscribers' Release Radar; if not, Spotify picks a track from the release itself. Re-releases of songs already on Spotify are not included in Release Radar.\[28\]
- **[Background: an observation by the agency Rise, 2025, not an individual artist's experience]** Since the start of 2025, Spotify seems to filter "alternate versions" (live, acoustic, karaoke) out of Release Radar by audio analysis, regardless of metadata and pitch. The agency noticed this with their artist, who released live recordings every Friday.\[30\] If you release live or acoustic versions, do not count on Release Radar even with a pitch.

**Where opinions differ:** some artists consider a pitch a "lottery", others a mandatory formality for the sake of Release Radar. The experience found supports the second: it is worth pitching every release at least to choose the track for Release Radar, but you cannot build a strategy on an editorial playlist.

### 5. Connection with other factors

- **How completely the pitch is filled in.** I found no user "A/B cases". **[Background]** Spotify writes: "More detail gives your song a better chance".\[2\]\[11\] The description field is limited to 500 characters.\[31\] Editors ask for the track's story, genre and vibe, and suitable playlists.\[20\] The Russian-language SAMESOUND, citing Soundcharts, advises mentioning blogs and radio airplay as proof that you are not a newcomer.\[16\]
- **Genre/tags and the "aliveness" of the target playlist.** The Future Funk case (@FuturePunk) shows that an exact tag leads to specific editors. But if their playlist is not updated, the pitch goes "nowhere". Before pitching, check whether the target playlist has been updated in recent weeks.
- **Artist history and a new profile.** The thibaultcroue case (2026): for a new artist, access to Spotify for Artists itself appears only after the first delivery, getting the URI and claiming the profile, and this eats up to several weeks.\[3\] Plan the first release of a new project with the largest margin. Another option: first release a "technical" track to get the profile in advance.
- **Pre-save / Countdown Pages.** No user cases with numbers were found. **[Background]** Spotify advises setting up a Countdown Page for pre-save and a track list preview for the sake of first-week streams.\[20\] Guides (Orphiq) put the Countdown Page/pre-save in the same window as the pitch: 3–4 weeks ahead.\[17\] The general conclusion is the same: early delivery is needed not only for the pitch.
- **Marquee / Discovery Mode / Showcase.** I could not find personal experience tied specifically to pitching. **[Background]** Spotify positions them as separate paid tools, but the launch timing on the official pages diverges: the help article "Getting started with Marquee and Showcase" gives "Within 21 days of release date" for Marquee and requires "at least 5,000 monthly active listeners" in the target market, the article "Creating a Marquee campaign" writes "Schedule your Marquee up to 18 days after the release date", and Showcase can be launched "Anytime". There are no claims about the influence of Discovery Mode on editorial selection in the sources found. Do not believe those who claim such a link exists unless they give data.
- **The pin in "This Is".** **[Background]** According to the Spotify help article "Pitching music to Spotify playlist editors", a pitched track can be pinned in the "This Is" playlist if you have one: "Songs are pinned to your This Is playlist for up to 28 days post-release", and the pinning itself after the track's release takes "around 3 days". This is one more bonus that only a pitch gives.

### 6. Can you pitch an already released track

**No.** The official rules and user experience agree on this.\[11\]\[22\]

- **iMusician Community (thread "Platforms problems – … Editorial Playlist Pitching").** To a group that was declined by the distributor's curators, support replied: reconsideration is impossible, and "at this stage it is no longer possible to pitch it to editorial playlists since it is already online".\[10\]
- **[Background: Spotify editors, 2020]** "While you can't pitch your music to us through Spotify for Artists post-release, our editors are definitely looking at signals from our data for songs that are resonating".\[32\] In other words, after release the only path is good metrics: saves, playlist adds, listen-through. Fresh Finds, according to a Spotify editor, is formed partly from an algorithmic list of "a thousand-ish tracks" a week plus pitches.\[33\]
- **What people try after release:** independent curators and paid pitching services. There is a risk here. Spotify Community users describe how tracks themselves landed in suspicious "repostng" and AI playlists: ~1500 streams, or +500 streams and ~50 followers overnight, after which the track was removed, and artists reported it to Spotify just in case.\[34\] If you want to "revive" a released track, use your own audience and vetted curators, not artificial boosting.
- **Re-uploading as a "new" track** does not help with Release Radar: Spotify does not include re-releases of already existing songs there **[background: help article]**.\[28\]

## Recommendations (a solution under the "one track" limit)

1. **Plan from the delivery date, not the pitch date.** Deliver 5–6 weeks ahead (a new artist: 6–7), and pitch on the same day the track appears in Upcoming. The target pitch window is 3–4 weeks before release; 7 days is only an emergency minimum.
2. **Singles one at a time, with a 5–6 week interval** (4 is the absolute minimum). Pitch the next track on the release day of the previous one.
3. **Do not keep two future releases in the system without need.** At least one user could not pitch one of them because of this.
4. **In an EP/album, pitch a track that has not yet been released as a single,** and write the pitch so that it is clear to the editor where to put it.
5. **Do not enable the distributor's paid pitch if you want to pitch yourself:** at iMusician these are mutually exclusive options, and on a decline the attempt is burned.\[24\]
6. **Check whether the target playlist is alive.** If it has not been updated for months, shift the emphasis of tags and description to neighboring playlists.
7. **Write the pitch in full right away.** Edits before release are possible, but Spotify does not guarantee that editors will see them.\[2\]
8. **Measure success soberly.** The guaranteed benefit is Release Radar (and the pin in "This Is"). An editorial playlist is a bonus, often personalized and with a moderate return.

## Limitations and contradictions

- **The main limitation:** I could not find real Reddit, Gearspace and KVR threads in the available index. User experience is taken from Spotify Community and iMusician Community; some of the cases are dated 2019–2021, and the rules may have changed since. The most recent and reliable cases are from iMusician, June and October 2026. I recommend supplementing the report with a manual search on Reddit inside the subreddits themselves (for example, "pitched 4 weeks Fresh Finds", "editorial playlist experience").
- **The "official vs practice" contradiction:** officially "7 days is enough", but in practice 7 days is often physically unattainable for a new artist. Officially "earlier is better", but I found no user evidence that a pitch 4+ weeks ahead raises the chance of landing in an editorial playlist. This is a reasonable hypothesis repeated by blogs, not a measured effect.
- **Outdated statistics:** "20% of pitches get playlisted" is data from the Spotify for Artists blog "Behind the Playlists" as of February 2020. There is no reason to think the odds are the same today, given the current volume of daily uploads.
- **Seasonality, Marquee, Discovery Mode, pre-save:** there is no evidence from personal experience. Everything written about them is guide advice not checked against cases.
